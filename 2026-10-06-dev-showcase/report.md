# What developers show in public, and does it pay? (sketch, 2026-10-06)

Public GitHub profile data only (no LinkedIn: its terms forbid automated collection).
"Open to work" = GitHub's own self-set `hireable` ("Available for hire"); "employed" = the
`company` field or "engineer at X" in the bio; "own work" = founder / indie / freelance in bio or company.

- **Writers:** 85 dev.to authors of popular articles (they listed a personal site, so "has a site" is ~100% by selection).
- **Builders:** 100 owners of repos with 10–5000 stars pushed in the last 7 days, whether they write or not.

Full numbers: results.txt. Rows: people.json.

## Findings
1. **Writers state their situation; builders mostly don't.** Writers: 42% employed, 24% own work, 34% not stated. Builders: 29% employed, 7% own work, 62% not stated.
2. **Writing goes with being "on the market".** 42% of writers tick "Available for hire" vs 24% of builders. Among writers doing their own work, 12 of 20 are hireable: freelancers and indie founders use public writing as their storefront.
3. **Builders show code, writers show text.** Median stars: builders 182, writers 46. 38% of builders have no link at all in their profile (vs 9% of writers); the repository is their showcase.
4. **LinkedIn is the writers' channel:** 55% of writers link it vs 17% of builders.
5. **Every extra link goes with more followers.** Median GitHub followers: 0 links 10, 1 link 44, 2 links 69, 4 links 121, 5 links 500. With a personal site/blog: 69 vs 14 without.
6. **Popularity is modest and long-tailed.** Median followers: writers 43, builders 48; means 290 and 643, driven by a few stars.

## Caveats
Correlation, not cause: popular people may simply add more links. Self-reported status; "not stated" is the biggest bucket.
Small samples; the builders' search favours recently updated mid-size repos.
